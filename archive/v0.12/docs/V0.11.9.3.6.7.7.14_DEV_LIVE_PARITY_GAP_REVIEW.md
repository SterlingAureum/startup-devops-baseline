# v0.11.9.3.6.7.7.14 aws-dev live parity gap review

Implementation baseline: `cd78d127968fc017364bf0775d84de0f4840cbe5`.

This checkpoint compares the complete `.7.7.13` eight-stage fixed-fake chain
with the closed public aws-dev teardown and residual-cost-audit evidence. It
does not reopen either historical approval and does not read the historical
private files.

## Outcome

The historical aws-dev run succeeded: its reviewed preflight matched, the
monolithic destroy wrapper exited successfully, Terraform reported 90 deleted
resources, post-success convergence passed, and the later residual-cost audit
found an empty Terraform state and no continuing cost identity. Those facts
remain valid historical evidence.

They are not equivalent to eight new live stage receipts. The old execution
predates the per-stage approval, saved-plan, state-transition and durable
receipt model. Its public evidence is intentionally aggregate and cannot be
expanded into object identities or stage outcomes that were not recorded.

Review result:

- eight of eight stages are complete under the fixed fake;
- zero of eight stages has an equivalent historical live receipt;
- ten historical controls are positively proven;
- eight structural gaps block a versioned dev live transport;
- historical approvals and synthetic receipts remain non-reusable;
- aws-dev, aws-test and aws-prod live execution remain disabled.

still incompatible with the new normal-path policy. A future versioned adapter
must not inherit that retry branch.

## Proven historical controls

The review preserves ten positive facts without granting current authority:

1. the historical execution was bound to an exact protected-main commit;
2. a reviewed private preflight fingerprint was bound;
3. the immediate preflight matched;
4. the target was exactly aws-dev;
5. a separate bounded approval was recorded;
6. the successful run performed no automatic retry;
7. private resource identities were not committed;
8. the later preflight observed an empty Terraform state;
9. the later audit found no continuing cost identity; and
10. aws-test was not created by the dev teardown.

These are historical outcome controls, not current execution inputs. Their old
approval text, clock window, state, proof and resource observations are expired
and cannot seed a future receipt chain.

## Blocking gaps

The following gaps must remain explicit:

- per-stage approval and fresh proof;
- per-stage terminal-success live receipts;
- per-stage state-before/state-after transitions;
- exact object identity plus independent postconditions;
- explicit ExternalSecret dependency drain;
- reviewed Terraform saved-plan byte binding;
- removal of the legacy automatic-retry capability; and
- a versioned closed live transport implementation.

Fresh private inputs, current pricing, budget and an execution window are also
required at execution time, but do not change the structural transport design.

## Validation boundary

The pure review accepts parsed public contracts and a versioned manifest only.
It performs no filesystem, clock, environment, subprocess, AWS, Kubernetes or
Terraform operation. The validator pins the old executor, destroy wrapper,
dependency-convergence helper, public teardown evidence, public residual audit
evidence and the `.7.7.13` chain contract by SHA-256.

Run:

```bash
bash scripts/validate-v0.11.9.3.6.7.7.14-dev-live-parity-gap-review.sh
```

The validator runs 39 negative and positive tests and recursively replays
`.7.7.13`. No live preflight, execute command, transport selector, endpoint,
credential reader or mutation authority is added.
